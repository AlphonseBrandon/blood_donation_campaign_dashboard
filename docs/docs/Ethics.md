# Blood Donation Eligibility Criteria  

## Overview  
Blood donation is a life-saving act that requires *strict eligibility criteria* to ensure the safety of both donors and recipients. These criteria, established by organizations such as the *World Health Organization (WHO, 2012), the American Red Cross (n.d.), the American Association of Blood Banks (AABB, n.d.), and national health services (NHS, n.d.)*, help prevent health risks and ensure the integrity of the blood supply.  

---

## Introduction
To maintain a safe and sustainable blood supply, donors must meet *specific health, demographic, and lifestyle requirements*. These standards help:  
1. *Protect donor health* by ensuring they can safely tolerate blood loss.  
2. *Prevent disease transmission* by screening for infections and medical conditions.  
3. *Maintain blood quality* by ensuring the suitability of donations (WHO, 2012).  

This document outlines *who is eligible* to donate, categorizing conditions into:  
- *Demographic eligibility*  
- *Medical eligibility (permanent and temporary deferrals)*  
- *Non-medical eligibility (lifestyle & exposure risks)*  

---

## 1. Demographic Eligibility  
- *Age:* 18–65 years (exceptions apply for younger donors with parental consent or older donors in good health) (WHO, 2012).  
- *Weight:* Minimum *50 kg (110 lbs)* to ensure adequate blood volume (American Red Cross, n.d.).  
- *Donation Frequency:*  
  - Whole blood: Every *8–12 weeks* (56 days for men, 84 days for women) (AABB, n.d.).  
  - Platelets & Plasma: Every *7–14 days*, depending on collection method (NHS, n.d.).  

---

## 2. Medical Eligibility  

### *2.1 Permanent Deferral (Lifetime Ineligibility)*  
These conditions pose a *high or long-term risk* to donor and recipient safety:  
- *Infectious Diseases:*  
  - HIV/AIDS, Hepatitis B/C (WHO, 2012).  
  - Creutzfeldt–Jakob Disease (CJD), Chagas disease, Babesiosis (AABB, n.d.).  
- *Chronic Illnesses:*  
  - *Severe cardiovascular conditions* (e.g., history of heart attack, stroke) (NHS, n.d.).  
  - *Advanced kidney disease, insulin-dependent diabetes with complications* (WHO, 2012).  
  - *Neurological disorders* (e.g., multiple sclerosis, dementia) (American Red Cross, n.d.).  
- *Cancer:*  
  - Blood cancers (e.g., leukemia, lymphoma) → Permanent deferral (WHO, 2012).  
  - Other cancers require *5 years in remission* before eligibility (AABB, n.d.).  
- *Blood Disorders:*  
  - Sickle cell disease, hemophilia requiring clotting factors (NHS, n.d.).  
- *Autoimmune Disorders:*  
  - Lupus, severe immunodeficiencies (WHO, 2012).  

---

### *2.2 Temporary Deferral (Short-Term Ineligibility)*  
Some conditions allow donation *after a recovery period*:  
- *Acute Illnesses & Infections:*  
  - Flu, fever, gastroenteritis → *Wait 14 days after full recovery* (WHO, 2012).  
  - Malaria risk → *Wait 6–12 months* after exposure (NHS, n.d.).  
- *Vaccinations:*  
  - Routine vaccines (e.g., flu, tetanus) → *48 hours deferral* (AABB, n.d.).  
  - Live virus vaccines (e.g., measles, hepatitis B) → *Up to 4 weeks* (WHO, 2012).  
- *Recent Surgeries & Medical Procedures:*  
  - *Minor surgeries (dental work, laparoscopic procedures)* → Wait *1–4 weeks* (American Red Cross, n.d.).  
  - *Major surgeries, blood transfusions* → Wait *6–12 months* (NHS, n.d.).  
- *Pregnancy & Breastfeeding:*  
  - *Pregnant individuals* → Deferred until *6 months post-delivery* (WHO, 2012).  
  - *Recent miscarriage/abortion* → Deferred *up to 6 months* (AABB, n.d.).  
- *Low Hemoglobin (Anemia):*  
  - Temporary deferral until levels normalize (American Red Cross, n.d.).  

---

## 3. Non-Medical Eligibility  

### *3.1 High-Risk Behaviors* (May Cause Permanent or Temporary Deferral)  
- *Intravenous drug use* → Permanent deferral (WHO, 2012).  
- *Sexual Behavior:*  
  - High-risk sexual activity → *3–12 month deferral*, depending on national policies (AABB, n.d.).  

### *3.2 Lifestyle & Exposure Risks*  
- *Tattooing, Piercing, or Acupuncture:*  
  - Performed in an unregulated facility → *12-month deferral* (WHO, 2012).  
  - In a licensed facility → *May require 4–6 month waiting period* (NHS, n.d.).  
- *Recent Travel to Malaria-Endemic Areas:*  
  - Deferral *6–12 months*, depending on exposure risk (WHO, 2012).  
- *Recent Incarceration or Exposure to High-Risk Environments:*  
  - Temporary deferral until risk assessment is completed (American Red Cross, n.d.).  

---

## Conclusion  
Blood donation is a critical resource for healthcare, but *donor eligibility is essential for safety. Strict guidelines ensure donations are **free from infectious risks* and that *donors remain healthy* after giving blood. Individuals unsure of their eligibility should consult *local blood donation services* for specific policies (WHO, 2012).
