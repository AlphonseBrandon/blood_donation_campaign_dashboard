# Medical Eligibility Criteria for Blood Donation

## Overview  
Blood donation is a vital process that saves lives, but it requires strict screening to ensure the safety of both donors and recipients. The eligibility criteria are based on guidelines from leading authorities such as the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Red Cross, the American Association of Blood Banks (AABB), and local health services (World Health Organization, 2017; American Red Cross, n.d.; AABB, n.d.; National Health Service, n.d.). These criteria help prevent the transmission of infections, safeguard donor health, and maintain a high-quality blood supply.

---

## Introduction  
To ensure the integrity and safety of the blood supply, international and local guidelines have established comprehensive medical criteria for donor eligibility. This document outlines these criteria in detail, focusing on key areas such as infectious diseases, cardiovascular health, post-surgical waiting periods, and chronic conditions. By adhering to these standards, blood donation services minimize health risks and promote a safe transfusion process.

---

## Medical Eligibility Criteria

### 1. Infectious Diseases  
- *HIV/AIDS and Viral Hepatitis:*  
  Individuals diagnosed with HIV or active hepatitis B or C are permanently deferred from donating blood. This precaution is essential to prevent the transmission of life-threatening infections (World Health Organization, 2017; American Red Cross, n.d.).

- *Other Infectious Diseases:*  
  Donors with active infections such as syphilis, tuberculosis, or malaria are temporarily deferred until they have fully recovered and completed the necessary treatment. For example, recovery from malaria typically requires a deferral period of 6 to 12 months (World Health Organization, 2017).

---

### 2. Cardiovascular Health  
- *Hypertension (High Blood Pressure):*  
  - *Controlled Hypertension:* Donors with well-managed blood pressure readings (typically below 180/100 mmHg) may be eligible to donate.  
  - *Uncontrolled Hypertension:* Those with uncontrolled blood pressure or newly diagnosed hypertension are deferred to prevent potential cardiovascular complications during donation (World Health Organization, 2017; National Health Service, n.d.).

- *Heart Disease and Stroke:*  
  A history of heart attacks, strokes, or recent cardiovascular surgeries generally results in permanent deferral due to the increased risk associated with blood loss (AABB, n.d.).

---

### 3. Post-Surgery and Recent Medical Treatments  
- *Surgical Procedures:*  
  - *Major Surgeries:* Individuals who have undergone major surgical procedures (e.g., organ transplants, major orthopedic surgeries) are typically deferred for 6 to 12 months to allow complete recovery.  
  - *Minor Surgeries:* For less invasive procedures, a shorter deferral period (usually 1 to 4 weeks) may be sufficient, depending on the procedure and recovery status (American Red Cross, n.d.).

- *Other Treatments:*  
  - *Blood Transfusions:* Donors who have recently received blood transfusions are deferred for 6 to 12 months to prevent the transmission of infections.  
  - *Cancer Treatments:* Individuals undergoing chemotherapy or radiation therapy are deferred for at least 12 months after treatment, with final eligibility determined by a healthcare professional (American Red Cross, n.d.).

---

### 4. Chronic Conditions and General Health Requirements  
- *Diabetes:*  
  Donors with well-controlled diabetes may be eligible. However, individuals on insulin therapy are evaluated on a case-by-case basis due to potential complications (National Health Service, n.d.).

- *Asthma and Respiratory Conditions:*  
  Controlled asthma generally does not disqualify a donor. However, severe or unstable respiratory conditions may lead to temporary or permanent deferral (American Red Cross, n.d.).

- *General Health Requirements:*  
  - *Weight:* Donors should typically weigh at least 50 kg (110 lbs) to ensure they have enough blood volume to safely donate.  
  - *Age:* The common age range for donation is between 18 and 65 years, with some exceptions for younger donors when parental consent is provided.  
  - *Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:* Pregnant individuals and those who have given birth within the past 6 months are deferred due to increased iron requirements and changes in blood volume (World Health Organization, 2017).

---

## Conclusion  
Adhering to the medical eligibility criteria for blood donation is essential for protecting both donors and recipients. The guidelines established by the WHO, the American Red Cross, the AABB, and local health services ensure that donated blood is safe and that donors are not exposed to undue risk. Regular updates and reviews of these standards help incorporate the latest medical research and maintain the highest level of care in blood transfusion services.

---

## Summary  
- *Infectious Diseases:* Permanent deferral for HIV/AIDS and active hepatitis; temporary deferral for other infections until recovery.  
- *Cardiovascular Health:* Controlled hypertension may be acceptable; severe heart conditions and recent cardiovascular events lead to deferral.  
- *Post-Surgery & Treatments:* Deferral periods range from 1-4 weeks for minor surgeries to 6-12 months for major surgeries and treatments.  
- *Chronic Conditions & General Health:* Conditions such as diabetes and asthma are assessed on stability, along with general requirements regarding weight, age, and pregnancy.
